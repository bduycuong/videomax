# ep28 — How much dead skin will you shed in your lifetime?

Facts: BBC Science Focus Q&A (sciencefocus.com/qa/how-much-skin-does-human-shed-their-life): 0.03–0.09 g of skin per hour (2011 Environmental Science & Technology study), about 35 kg in a lifetime, "roughly half your bodyweight". Live Science ("Is house dust mostly dead skin?", citing the American Chemical Society): same 0.03–0.09 g/h, ~500 million cells/day; a 2009 US Midwest dust study found ~60 % of house dust from indoor sources (fibres, building materials, skin…) and ~40 % tracked/blown in from outdoors. Cleveland Clinic (Skin): "new skin every 30 days" (outer layer renews about monthly).
Arithmetic: 0.06 g/h (mid of range) × 24 = 1.44 g/day ("about one and a half grams"); × 365 = 525.6 g/yr ("about half a kilo"); × 80 yr = 42.0 kg ("about forty kilos"; BBC says ~35 kg, low end 0.03 g/h → 21 kg, high end 0.09 g/h → 63 kg). 40 kg ≈ half of an ~80 kg adult. Viral "2,880 kg" = 3 kg of whole skin × 12 months × 80 years — wrong, only the thin outer layer sheds. 2D only (no three.js: no natural 3D hero, CPU budget).
