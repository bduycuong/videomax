# ep36 — Is the Moon running away from Earth?

Facts: NASA Goddard eclipse site "Measuring the Moon's Distance" + NASA JPL "Apollo 11 experiment continues to return valuable data" (Apollo-era retroreflectors, lunar laser ranging, Moon recedes about 3.8 cm / 1.5 in per year, ocean tides raised by the Moon affect its orbit); Space.com physicist explainer (bulge dragged ahead of the Moon by Earth's spin pulls it forward into a larger orbit, Earth's spin loses the same momentum so days lengthen; the Moon will not escape; in roughly a billion years the brightening Sun boils off the oceans that make the bulge); NASA Space Place "How far away is the Moon?" (average 384,400 km).
Arithmetic (Python): 3.8 cm x 80 years = 304 cm ≈ 3 m; 3 m / 384,400 km ≈ 8 parts per billion; 1 km takes ≈ 26,000 years.
3D: hook (Earth + Moon on a widening dotted orbit) and tides (top-down Earth with ocean bulge dragged ahead of the Moon).
