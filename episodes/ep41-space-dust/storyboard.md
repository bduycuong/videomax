# ep41 — How much space dust falls on Earth every year?

Facts: Rojas et al. 2021, Earth and Planetary Science Letters 566:116794 (doi 10.1016/j.epsl.2021.116794), "The micrometeorite flux at Dome C (Antarctica)": ~20 years of collecting near Concordia station; global micrometeorite flux ≈ 5,200 t/yr (12–700 µm); most from Jupiter-family comets. CNRS press release (8 Apr 2021) "More than 5,000 tons of extraterrestrial dust fall to Earth each year": 5,200 t/yr, meteorites < 10 t/yr, ~80% from comets, rest asteroids, grains 30–200 µm. Genge et al., Geology 45(2) 2017 (online Dec 2016) "An urban collection of modern-day large micrometeorites": ~500 micrometeorites from urban rooftops (Project Stardust gutter sediment, Oslo and Paris per Sky & Telescope).
Arithmetic (Python): 5,200 / 365 = 14.2 t/day; / 24 = 594 kg/h; / 60 = 9.9 kg/min ≈ 10 kg.
3D: hook (toon Earth, seeded dust streaks diving in) and answer (camera pull-back from a close patch to the whole Earth while dust rains in).
