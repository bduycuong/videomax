# ep34 — Why is the ocean salty, but rivers aren't?

Facts: USGS FAQ "Why is the ocean salty?" + USGS Water Science School "Why is the ocean salty?" (rain absorbs CO2 -> weak carbonic acid -> weathers rock; dissolved ions travel runoff -> streams -> rivers -> ocean; rivers carry too little salt to taste and keep flowing; evaporation removes water, salt stays and builds up over hundreds of millions of years; hydrothermal vents / undersea volcanoes add minerals; salinity ~35 ppt = ~3.5 % = ~35 g per liter; sodium + chloride > 90 % of ions; spread over land the salt would be > 500 ft thick, about a 40-story building).
Arithmetic (Python): 35 g / ~17 g per tablespoon of table salt = ~2 tablespoons; 500 ft = 152 m; 500 ft / 40 stories = 12.5 ft per story (typical office floor). 2D only (no natural 3D hero worth +2 min on a shared 2-CPU box).
