# ep33 — Why do your ears pop on a plane?

Facts: Mayo Clinic "Airplane ear" (eardrum stress when middle-ear pressure does not match cabin pressure; Eustachian tube links middle ear to back of nose/throat; swallowing or yawning opens it; tips: yawn, swallow, chew gum, gentle Valsalva, stay awake on takeoff/landing, babies bottle/pacifier; see a doctor if symptoms last more than a few days or are severe). Medscape "Eustachian Tube Function" (adult tube ~36 mm; closed at rest, opens with swallowing/yawning; on descent relative negative middle-ear pressure can lock the tube shut). SKYbrary "Cabin Altitude" (cabin normally kept at 8,000 ft or less).
Arithmetic (Python, barometric formula): 8,000 ft = 2,438 m -> p/p0 = 0.743 -> ~26 % less ("about a quarter less"). 36 mm ~ "about three and a half centimeters" (on screen 3.5 cm). 2D only (2-core CPU, deadline).
