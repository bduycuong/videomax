# ep29 — Why do we hiccup?

Facts: Mayo Clinic (hiccups = involuntary spasms of the diaphragm; each one makes the vocal cords close suddenly, giving the "hic"; triggers include carbonated drinks, overeating/eating fast, excitement; most last a few minutes; see a provider if they last more than 48 hours). UCL press release Nov 2019 (via EurekAlert mirror of the UCL release; UCL page itself was not reachable here): fetal hiccups from about 9 weeks; preterm infants hiccup about 1% of their time (~15 min/day); 13 newborns studied; each hiccup evokes three brainwaves (two large + a third); idea: helps the baby's brain learn to monitor breathing muscles. Gill hypothesis: Straus et al. 2003, BioEssays, "A phylogenetic hypothesis for the origin of hiccough" — presented only as one idea ("nobody knows for sure").
Arithmetic: 1% of 24 h = 14.4 min ≈ "about fifteen minutes a day". 48 h = "two days" (on screen "2 days"). Home remedies: no claims, "evidence is thin". 2D only (no three.js — 2-core CPU, no natural 3D hero).
